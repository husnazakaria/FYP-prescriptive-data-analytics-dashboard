Problem Landscape & Macroeconomic Reality

Core Financial Challenges (Chapter 1)

  • Income Stagnation: Avg RM 2,456/month for fresh graduates — RM 2,874 below DOSM 2024 national mean of RM 5,330 (gap widened from RM 2,515 in 2020)
  • DSR Crisis: 37 respondents breach BNM 60% critical DSR threshold
  • Geographic Pressure: Only 2 of 16 Malaysian states affordable for fresh graduates (coverage ≥70%)
  • Tool Gap: Existing tools only descriptive — no prescriptive action guidance

50/30/20 Rule — Core Prescriptive Framework

  • 50% Needs — Essential spending limit (rent, food, transport)
  • 30% Wants — Lifestyle spending limit (dining, leisure)
  • 20% Savings — Minimum monthly savings target
  • Compliance: 78% Needs ✓ · 48% Savings ✗ · 82% Wants ✓

Why Rule-Based DAX over Machine Learning?
  • 100% Explainable (White-Box): Users understand exactly why each recommendation is generated
  • BNM Compliant: DSR thresholds (40% warning, 60% critical) mathematically locked in DAX
  • DOSM Validated: All benchmarks grounded in DOSM 2024 official statistics
  • Academic Audit-Ready: Evaluators can fully trace every decision path
  • No Black-Box Risk: Unlike ML, every threshold is documented and justifiable
  • User Trust: Transparency = higher likelihood of behavioural compliance

P1 — Dashboard Overview: Financial Risk by Age Group, Awareness Breakdown, Alerts

What This Page Shows

4 KPI cards: Total Respondents (100), Avg Awareness (4.00), Avg Income (RM 2.4K), High-Risk Users (20%). Stacked bar showing RAG distribution by age group. Donut showing awareness level split. 50/30/20 compliance metrics. Top financial alert bar chart.

Key Finding

20% of graduates are in the financial Danger Zone (3+ alerts). 51% are in Warning. Despite high awareness (62% High Awareness), 52% fail the savings target — confirming the Knowledge-Action Gap.

Why It Matters

Establishes the core thesis: financial awareness ≠ financial action. The executive overview proves that descriptive analytics alone are insufficient — prescriptive tools are needed.

FYP Objective

Objective 1 — Descriptive analytics. Maps the scale of the financial vulnerability problem across 97 Malaysian fresh graduates.

P2 — Demographics: Income, Employment, Location, Qualification, Awareness by Income

What This Page Shows

Monthly income distribution (62% earn below RM 2,500). Employment status by gender (55.4% female Full-Time). Qualification donut (61% Degree). State distribution (51% in Selangor & W.P. KL). Financial awareness score by income band.

Key Finding

62% earn below RM 2,500/month. Financial awareness scores are fairly uniform across income bands (3.9–4.3/5, near ceiling) — indicating income level, not lack of financial knowledge, is the primary driver of financial vulnerability.

Why It Matters

Pins the target demographic: young (69% aged 24 and below), low-income, urban graduates concentrated in the highest-cost states. Justifies a geographically-sensitive prescriptive approach.

FYP Objective

Objective 1 — Diagnostic analytics. Identifies demographic drivers of financial risk for targeted prescriptive intervention.

P3 — Financial Health: Decomposition Tree, Gauges, Savings vs Income Scatter

What This Page Shows

Decomposition Tree: "Why Are Graduates Struggling?" — drill down by Employment → Income → Education → Gender. Two gauges: Needs Allocation (38.8% vs 50% limit) and Savings Rate (23.4% vs 20% target). Scatter: savings rate vs essential spending per respondent.

Key Finding

Savings compliance only 48% — 52% fail the 20% target. Needs compliance 78% (22% overspend on essentials). The decomposition tree shows Full-Time Employed graduates earning RM 2,501–RM 3,500 with a Degree score highest awareness (4.09), confirming income level — not education — is the root cause of financial stress.

The decomposition tree is unique diagnostic proof — it visually isolates root causes of financial stress, directly supporting the research argument that structural income issues drive the Knowledge-Action Gap.

Key Finding

Salary gap widened: RM 2,515 (2020) → RM 2,874 (2024). Graduates aged 24 and below suffer the highest economic vulnerability, exhibiting a deficit of −RM 66 vs DOSM relative to national baselines. Despite falling unemployment (4.4% → 3.2%), fresh graduate financial stress is increasing — employment ≠ financial security.

Why It Matters

Provides the macroeconomic evidence that the problem is structural (national wage stagnation) not behavioral (individual poor choices). Justifies prescriptive intervention as a systemic policy need.

FYP Objective

Objective 1 — Diagnostic analytics using DOSM secondary data. Validates hybrid data approach combining primary survey with national benchmarks.

User Acceptance Testing (UAT)
UAT

30 Malaysian fresh graduates interacted with the fully deployed platform (all 8 dashboard pages + Web Portal) for 20–30 minutes before completing the instrument. 53.3% male / 46.7% female; 53.3% earning below RM 1,500/month.

Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), extended to 5 constructs: Perceived Ease of Use (PEU), Perceived Usefulness (PU), Attitude Toward System (ATT), Behavioural Intention (BI), System Quality (SQ) · 22 Likert items (Q6–Q27) · Mode as sole measure of central tendency
